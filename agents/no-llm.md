# Writing for humans (no LLM-sounding prose)

Use this guide when you write or edit prose that a person will read: PR
descriptions, design docs, READMEs, Slack messages and canvases, review
comments and summaries. It doesn't apply to code, commit trailers or
machine-readable output.

The goal isn't to fool a detector. It's to respect the reader's time and
attention, so they don't scroll away thinking it's AI slop.

For substantial drafts, work in three passes. Don't skip any of them. For short
messages, apply the same checks mentally before sending.

## Pass 1: audit

Read the draft and list every place you find:

- Overused AI vocabulary (see the list below).
- Consecutive sentences that all fall in the same length range.
- Consecutive paragraphs that follow the topic sentence, explanation, example,
  wrap-up pattern.
- Hedging or "both sides" framing where a real position would be clearer.
- Triads (groups of three adjectives or phrases) used for rhythm.
- Smooth transition words that exist mostly to look organized.
- Generic phrases where a concrete detail would land better.

## Pass 2: critique

For each finding, write one sentence on what's wrong and what the writing needs
instead. Don't rewrite yet.

## Pass 3: rewrite

Rewrite the full text using these principles.

### Voice

- Sound like a thoughtful person sharing what they've learned, not a brand
  voice or a corporate report.
- Take real positions. If the source hedges, find the actual point underneath
  and say it gently but clearly.
- Use contractions naturally (it's, don't, you're, we'll).
- Use active voice unless passive genuinely sounds better.

### Rhythm

- Vary sentence length a lot. Mix short sentences (3 to 8 words) with longer
  ones (25 or more) in the same paragraph.
- Don't let three sentences in a row have a similar length or shape.
- It's fine to start a sentence with "And" or "But".
- Fragments are okay. Use them sparingly.
- Ellipses (…) are welcome where a gentle pause helps.

### Specificity

- Replace generic claims with concrete ones from the source. "A recent study"
  becomes the actual study. "Many users" becomes a number if there is one.
- Cut filler that doesn't carry information.
- Match detail to the reader. Leave out versions, SKUs and protocol settings
  they don't need in order to understand or act.
- Prefer plain words, and don't repeat a distinctive verb (for example
  "steer").

### Never use

- "what no one else is talking about" or "what nobody tells you"
- loop, signal, cascade, drift or drifting
- quiet or quietly
- tapestry, realm, landscape (as metaphors)
- in conclusion, ultimately, to summarize
- unlock, unleash, harness
- "not just X but Y" or "it's not X, it's Y" framing
- Em-dashes (use commas, parentheses, or split the sentence)

When a banned word is a real technical term (for example Temporal Signals or a
control loop), reword it if you can without losing meaning. If you can't, keep
the term and say so in the note to the author.

### Protect

- Keep every fact, figure, name and claim from the source. Don't add new
  information or examples, and don't drop any.
- Stay within about 10% of the original length.

## Output

After the rewrite, add one short paragraph for the author (not the reader)
noting the biggest changes and why.
